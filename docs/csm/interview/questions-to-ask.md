# Questions to ask them

Ask three to five, chosen for the stage. Good questions are the cheapest way to read as a
peer rather than a candidate — and several of these will genuinely tell you whether the
job is worth taking.

## Best five (ask these)

**1. "How do you measure customer health today, and how much do you trust it?"**
The second clause is what makes it good. Every company says they have a health score;
few trust it. The answer tells you whether the CS function is instrumented or improvised —
and it sets you up to talk about building one.

**2. "What does the CSM own that the account executive doesn't — specifically at renewal?"**
The single most important question about the job. If CS carries the renewal number, this
is a quota role wearing a CS title. If AE owns it, you need to know how much influence you
have without the commercial lever. Ambiguity here is the most common reason CSMs leave.

**3. "What's the ratio — how many accounts and how much ARR per CSM?"**
Concrete and revealing. High ratios mean pooled, reactive work regardless of what the JD
promises. It also signals you have run a real book and know the number that matters.

**4. "Tell me about a customer you lost in the last year. What actually happened?"**
Tests their honesty and their diagnostic culture. A team that can narrate a churn clearly
learns from it; a team that blames the customer or the product does not. The quality of
this answer predicts your experience there more than anything on the JD.

**5. "When CS raises a recurring product problem, what happens to it?"**
Straight from Theme 4 and your Story 4. If the answer is vague, CS is a complaint sink
rather than a voice in the roadmap — which is exactly the job you would be signing up for.

## Good follow-ups by stage

**Recruiter**
- What does the interview process look like end to end, and what's the timeline?
- Is this a new headcount or a backfill? *(Backfill: ask why they left.)*
- How is the CS team structured — by segment, region, or industry?

**Hiring manager**
- What would a successful first 90 days look like?
- What's the hardest part of this job that isn't in the description?
- How is the team split between onboarding and ongoing management?
- Who did well in this role before, and what made them good at it?

**Skip-level / leadership**
- Where does CS sit — under revenue, product, or its own function? *(Tells you your budget and influence.)*
- What is the company's biggest retention risk over the next year?
- How has the CS function changed in the last two years?

**Peer / cross-functional**
- What do you wish CS did differently?
- How do escalations actually work here, versus how they are documented?

## Two to ask about your own gaps

Asked well, these turn weaknesses into engagement:

- **"Which CS platform do you run, and how much of the role is administering it?"**
  Surfaces the tooling gap on your terms rather than theirs, and the answer tells you how
  much ramp you would need.
- **"How much of this role is expansion versus retention?"**
  Lets you calibrate before they ask you for an upsell story you have not yet written.

## Do not ask

- Anything answered on the careers page or in the JD — it reads as not having read it.
- Salary or leave policy at the hiring-manager stage. That is a recruiter conversation.
- "What does a typical day look like?" — too passive, and you are senior enough that it
  reads as junior.
- "Do you have any concerns about my candidacy?" — invites a negative framing you then
  have to argue out of. If you want the same information, ask what the strongest candidates
  they have seen bring.
