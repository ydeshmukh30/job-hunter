# Likely questions — Customer Success Manager

Grouped by interview stage. Each question notes which story from `star-stories.md` answers
it, so you are reusing six prepared stories rather than improvising thirty answers.

---

## Stage 1 — Recruiter screen (20–30 min)

Filtering, not evaluating. Be concise and hit the keywords.

| Question | Approach |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through your background." | 90 seconds, chronological, land on *enterprise customer success*. Say "SaaS" and "enterprise" out loud. |
| "Why are you looking?" | Forward-looking, never critical of Superset. Segment, scale, or industry you want next. |
| "What's your current book?" | `[[NEED: account count + ARR]]`. Know this cold — hesitating here reads as not owning your accounts. |
| "Salary expectations?" | `[[NEED: researched range]]`. Deflect once ("what's the band for the role?"), give a range if pressed. |
| "Notice period?" | `[[NEED]]`. In India this is often the real scheduling constraint — have the exact number. |
| "Tell me about the nine-month gap." | Straightforward: focused time to complete the MBA, then returned into an enterprise CS role. No apology, no over-explanation. |
| "What CS tools do you use?" | See Gap 2. Name real tools. "Internal platform, previously X and Y" is a strong answer. |

## Stage 2 — Hiring manager (45–60 min)

Where the role is won. Almost every question maps to one of the four themes.

**Theme 1 — relationship ownership**
- "How do you build a relationship with a new enterprise account?" → Story 1 + Story 2
- "Tell me about a difficult customer." → **Story 2**
- "How do you handle a customer who has gone quiet?" → **Story 3** — the silence-predicts-churn point is your best line here
- "What do you do when a customer asks for something we cannot deliver?" → honesty on timelines, workaround without pretending it is a fix (Story 4's follow-up)
- "How do you prioritise across a large book?" → **Story 3**, risk-based segmentation

**Theme 2 — retention & metrics**
- "What retention did you hold?" → `[[NEED]]`. Non-negotiable.
- "How do you know an account is at risk before it churns?" → engagement signals, unresolved-issue silence, stakeholder change. Then: "at Superset I watch `[[NEED: your actual signals]]`."
- "What metrics do you report to leadership, and how often?" → `[[NEED]]`
- "How would you build a health score from scratch?" → strong question for you: usage frequency, breadth of adoption, support-contact sentiment, stakeholder stability, contract proximity. Say you would validate it against accounts that already churned.
- "Walk me through a renewal you nearly lost." → `[[NEED: prepare one]]`

**Theme 3 — onboarding & adoption**
- "How do you run an onboarding?" → **Story 1**. Your strongest answer in the whole interview.
- "How do you drive adoption when the champion leaves?" → multi-threading; you should have hit this at Superset when placement officers rotate
- "How do you measure time-to-value?" → first-milestone definition, then days-to-milestone
- "Tell me about training you have delivered." → **Story 1**, bootcamps and townhalls

**Theme 4 — cross-functional & commercial**
- "How do you get product to prioritise your customer's issue?" → **Story 4**. Patterns, not complaints.
- "Tell me about influencing without authority." → **Story 4**
- "How do you identify expansion opportunities?" → **Gap 3 — you need Story 7. Write it.**
- "Tell me about a negotiation." → **Story 6**, framed in one sentence as pre-SaaS commercial experience

**Behavioural staples**
- "Tell me about a failure." → have one ready and own it fully; the recovery is the answer
- "Where do you see yourself in three years?" → Director of CS, or CS leadership. Ambition is fine in this role.
- "Why this company?" → the same research the cover-letter hook needs

## Stage 3 — Case / role-play

Common formats and how to handle them:

**"Your largest account just sent a churn notice. Walk me through your first week."**
Do not jump to saving it. Sequence: understand *why* first — call the champion, then the
economic buyer separately, because they often give different answers. Diagnose whether it
is value, price, a people change, or an unresolved grievance. Only then build a case. Say
out loud that some churn is correct and a good CSM knows which.

**"Onboard me. I am a new enterprise customer."**
Story 1, live. Discovery on their success criteria before any product talk. Define first
milestone. Name the stakeholders and multi-thread. Set the review cadence.

**"Here is usage data for five accounts. Which do you worry about?"**
They want to see if you read data or narrate it. Look for declining breadth of adoption
(fewer features, fewer users) rather than declining volume, and for single-user
concentration — one power user is a churn risk when they leave.

**"An angry customer emails your VP about you. Respond."**
Acknowledge without defensiveness, take it offline to a call, fix the substance, then
close the loop with the VP. Never argue in writing.

## Questions you should expect to struggle with

Be honest with yourself about these three and prepare them properly:

1. **"What was your NRR / gross retention?"** — you do not have the number documented yet.
2. **"Which CS platform have you administered?"** — Gap 2.
3. **"Tell me about an account you expanded."** — Gap 3, no story yet.

All three are answerable. None are answerable *well* without preparation.
