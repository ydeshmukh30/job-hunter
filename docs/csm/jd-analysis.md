# JD analysis — Customer Success Manager

The posting lists 13 responsibilities and 20 qualifications. They collapse into four
things the role is actually testing. Structure every resume bullet, story and answer
against these four, not against the 13.

## Theme 1 — Own the relationship, not the ticket

> "primary contact" · "engaging proactively" · "advocating for customer needs" ·
> "managing customer lifecycle and escalations"

The distinction being drawn is reactive support vs. proactive ownership. A support rep
answers what is asked; a CSM notices what has not been asked yet. Interviewers probe
this with "tell me about a time you spotted a problem before the customer raised it."

**Vanshika's strength.** Direct hit. Currently *Manager, Enterprise Customer Success* —
the exact title, at the exact segment the JD's "complex accounts" language implies. The
Multiplier APAC remit and 500+ named relationships at WhiteHat Jr give a decade-shaped
progression from volume relationship management to enterprise ownership.

## Theme 2 — Retention as a measured outcome

> "ensuring high retention" · "monitoring health metrics, tracking KPIs" ·
> "analyze customer data and usage trends" · "reporting outcomes to leadership"

This is the theme that separates candidates. The JD asks for retention *and* the ability
to instrument it — health scores, usage trends, KPI reporting upward. Most applicants
claim the first and cannot evidence the second.

**Vanshika's strength.** Partial. "Surpassing KPIs", "driving critical business metrics",
"improving Net Promoter Scores" all appear in her own words, so the instinct is there and
documented. **The numbers behind them are not.** This is the single biggest gap and the
one most worth closing before applying — see `gap-analysis.md`.

## Theme 3 — Onboarding, enablement and adoption

> "onboarding new clients and guiding product setup and training" ·
> "delivering training sessions" · "drive adoption and maximize value" ·
> "creating customized reports"

Time-to-value work. Underrated by applicants, heavily weighted by hiring managers,
because a CSM who onboards well prevents the churn everyone else spends the year fighting.

**Vanshika's strength.** Unusually strong, and under-marketed on her current profile.
"Operations & **Training** Manager" at a SaaS company, building SOPs and checklists from
scratch across three regions, running bootcamps and townhalls. That is enablement
infrastructure, not just delivery. Very few CSM applicants can say they *built* the
onboarding system rather than executed someone else's.

## Theme 4 — Cross-functional influence without authority

> "communicating product improvements to development" · "collaborating with internal
> teams" · "understanding of product management's impact on customer experience" ·
> "negotiation skills for complex accounts"

Can you make engineering care about your customer's problem? The JD explicitly asks for
understanding of *product management's* impact — unusual phrasing, and a signal they want
someone who has sat in that loop.

**Vanshika's strength.** Evidenced. "Coordinating closely with the product team to ensure
seamless on-floor operations" at saasguru, plus an MBA in **Project Management** which
maps directly onto the cross-functional coordination language. Sponsor negotiation and
ticketing-platform partnerships at JSM cover the negotiation requirement from an angle
most CS candidates cannot.

## Positioning

Lead with the **SaaS operator who builds the system**, not the account babysitter. Three
consecutive SaaS companies, one as a founding member, with the onboarding and enablement
infrastructure built by hand — against a field of applicants who have only ever worked
inside someone else's playbook. Retention numbers, once supplied, make this a strong
application rather than a good one.
