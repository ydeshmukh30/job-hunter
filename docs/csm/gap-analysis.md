# Gap analysis — Vanshika Tewary vs. the CSM JD

Honest read. Four real gaps, ordered by how much they cost and how cheaply they close.
Three of the four are *documentation* gaps rather than capability gaps — which is good
news, because those close in a conversation rather than a year.

---

## Gap 1 — No quantified retention outcomes · **severity: high · cost to close: one hour**

**The gap.** The JD asks for retention, health metrics, KPI tracking and reporting to
leadership. Vanshika's profile claims all four qualitatively — "surpassing KPIs", "driving
critical business metrics", "improving Net Promoter Scores" — and quantifies none of them.
The only hard numbers anywhere are 500+ customers, 35–40 artists per week, and a 9/10 GPA.

**Why it matters more than anything else here.** CSM screening is metric-driven. A resume
that says "improved customer satisfaction" and one that says "lifted NPS from 31 to 48 in
two quarters" describe the same work, and only the second gets a call. Recruiters read the
first as a candidate who was never close enough to the numbers to know them.

**How to close it.** Vanshika already has these numbers or can retrieve them. Needed:

- Logo or revenue retention across her book at Superset
- Account count, and ARR if shareable
- NPS before → after at saasguru
- CSAT movement at Multiplier
- Renewal rate at WhiteHat Jr across the 500+ book

If exact figures are unavailable or confidential, defensible ranges work: "held retention
above 90%", "roughly 40 enterprise accounts". What does *not* work is inventing a number —
it will not survive the second interview.

---

## Gap 2 — No named CRM or customer success platform · **severity: high · cost: one answer**

**The gap.** "Familiarity with CRM tools and customer success platforms" is a *required*
qualification. Gainsight, ChurnZero and HubSpot are named under preferred. Vanshika's
profile names no tools at all, despite five years in roles that certainly used them.

**Why it matters.** These are the most literally string-matched terms in any ATS. Six of
the forty-five keywords in `keyword-map.md` are blocked on this single question. A
recruiter filtering for "Salesforce" will not surface this profile today.

**How to close it.** List what she has actually used, however unglamorous — Zoho, Freshdesk,
Intercom, Zendesk, HubSpot, a homegrown admin panel. Breadth is not required; naming
something is. If the honest answer is that Superset runs on an internal tool, say that:
"internal CS platform; previously Zendesk and HubSpot" is a perfectly strong line.

---

## Gap 3 — Upsell and cross-sell not evidenced · **severity: medium · cost: one story**

**The gap.** "Identifying upselling and cross-selling opportunities" is an explicit
responsibility. Nothing on the profile speaks to expansion revenue.

**Why it matters.** It is the one responsibility where Vanshika's positioning — the
operations-and-enablement builder — could read as a weakness. An interviewer may wonder
whether she is a delivery person rather than a commercial one.

**How to close it.** Almost certainly a documentation gap, not a real one: an enterprise
CSM at a hiring platform will have expanded accounts into new departments, campuses or
seat tiers. One concrete example is enough. Note that the JD itself hedges — it says the
role "focuses on client retention and satisfaction rather than sales-driven revenue
generation" — so this needs one solid story, not a quota history. There is a slot for it
in `interview/star-stories.md`.

---

## Gap 4 — Bachelor's degree not shown · **severity: low–medium · cost: one line**

**The gap.** "Bachelor's degree in Business, Marketing, Communications, or related field"
is listed as required. The LinkedIn profile shows only the MBA.

**Why it probably does not matter.** An MBA with 9/10 from an accredited European
university satisfies the intent, and most screeners will treat it as exceeding rather than
missing the bar. But some ATS configurations filter on the bachelor's field specifically.

**How to close it.** Add it to both the resume and LinkedIn. If the field is unrelated to
business, list it plainly and let the MBA carry the relevance — this is not worth
explaining or apologising for.

---

## Not gaps, despite appearances

**The nine-month career break** (Apr–Dec 2024). Explained, purposeful, and already
labelled on the profile. Completing an MBA is among the best possible reasons for a gap.
Leave it visible. If asked, the honest answer — "I took focused time to finish my MBA and
came back into an enterprise CS role" — is a good answer, not a defensive one.

**The Event Manager role.** It looks off-track next to four SaaS roles, but it supplies
the sponsor and vendor **negotiation** experience the JD asks for and most CS candidates
lack entirely. Keep one line of it. Do not lead with it.

**No CSM certification.** Listed as preferred, not required, and easily the cheapest gap
on this page to close if she wants it — SuccessHACKER or a Gainsight Associate credential
is days, not months. Worth doing only if two otherwise-equal applications are competing;
the enterprise title matters far more.

---

## Summary

The underlying profile is a strong match — three consecutive SaaS companies, current
enterprise CS title, founding-team operational depth, and an MBA. What is weak is the
*evidence*, not the experience. Closing Gaps 1 and 2 turns this from a decent application
into a competitive one, and both close in a single conversation with the candidate.

**Do not apply until Gaps 1 and 2 are closed.** They are worth more than any further
rewriting of the documents already in this branch.
